\colorbox{red}{\underline{1:}} How many ways can $5$ differently colored balls be placed into bins labeled $1 - 8$ so that no bin contains more than one ball?
\begin{solution}
    We first choose which $5$ of the $8$ bins will be occupied and then multiply by the number of different ways to arrange the $5$ balls:
    \begin{align*}
        \binom{8}{5} \cdot 5! = \boxed{6720}
    \end{align*}
\end{solution}

Thirty items are arranged in a 6-by-5 array as shown below:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
$A_1$  & $A_2$  & $A_3$  & $A_4$  & $A_5$  \\ \hline

$A_{11}$ & $A_{12}$ & $A_{13}$ & $A_{14}$ & $A_{15}$ \\ \hline
$A_{16}$ & $A_{17}$ & $A_{18}$ & $A_{19}$ & $A_{20}$ \\ \hline
$A_{21}$ & $A_{22}$ & $A_{23}$ & $A_{24}$ & $A_{25}$ \\ \hline
$A_{26}$ & $A_{27}$ & $A_{28}$ & $A_{29}$ & $A_{30}$ \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Calculate the number of ways to form a set of three distinct items such that no two of the selected items are in the same row or column.
\begin{solution}
    We first choose the row, then the associated column, and then account for the different ways to pair them together:
    \[
        \binom{5}{3} \cdot \binom{6}{3} \cdot 3! = \frac{5!}{3!(5-3)!} \cdot \frac{6!}{3!(6-3)!} \cdot 3! = \boxed{1200}  
    \]
\end{solution}
